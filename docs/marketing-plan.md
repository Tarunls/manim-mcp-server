# Orune: a practical first month of marketing

Research and recommendations, September 7, 2026. The experiments below are recommendations, not campaigns already launched or proven conversion rates.

## Positioning

Lead with the work people can make: animated explanations they can review and refine. The current product is particularly demonstrable in mathematics: area accumulation, derivatives, waves and geometry. Start with independent STEM tutors, teachers making reusable explanations, and small educational creators. Students are a useful discovery audience, but recurring lesson production is a stronger initial hypothesis for paid demand. Test that hypothesis in conversations.

Show a real prompt, its rendered result, and one visible correction. Frame annotation is a more specific reason to choose Orune than another promise about generating content with AI. Do not imply perfect mathematical accuracy, guaranteed duration, instant generation, unlimited usage, or that the current Free tier includes narration. Describe AI assistance plainly when asked; emphasize the reviewed output in promotional work.

## Homepage changes in this branch

The warm paper, serif display type, restrained colors, circle-to-triangle hero and three animated demonstrations stay. The supporting sentence explains the product and frame feedback. A three-step row now answers the navigation's “How it works” link. A separate sample player offers three existing videos. Mobile visitors can reach navigation links. The resonance canvas can be paused, pauses when offscreen, and starts still for reduced-motion preferences; the other existing visuals retain their own motion behavior. Lower images reserve their dimensions and load lazily; share metadata describes the product directly.

These decisions follow NN/g's emphasis on immediately explaining a site's purpose and giving visitors a clear next action. They are usability improvements, not a forecast of higher conversion. [Homepage design principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/). Motion preferences also need a meaningful alternative. [web.dev guidance](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion).

## Four experiments, in order

| When | Work to do | What to learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Make three strong, reviewed sample lessons and three short recordings showing prompt → result → one annotation edit. Talk individually to 10 STEM educators/creators; ask about a concept they already need to explain. | Does the result save preparation time? Can someone reach a useful first video? |
| Week 2 | Publish three short teaching clips and one longer walkthrough using actual exports. Every clip should teach one concept before asking viewers to try Orune. | Which subject attracts people who actually finish a generation? |
| Week 3 | Help five willing educators make a lesson for their real class or channel. Ask permission to show their work and record an honest quote. Offer a bounded amount of help/credits only if you can support it. | Does a successful first video lead to a second lesson and willingness to pay? |
| Week 4 | Repeat the topic and format that produced returning creators. Publish a case study with their permission. Consider a small paid test only if conversion and generation cost are understood. | Which audience has enough repeat value to acquire economically? |

Recruiting early users personally and helping them succeed is a useful founder strategy, rather than waiting for launch traffic to convert itself. [Paul Graham on early user recruitment](https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html).

## Promotional formats

- Teach something visually: show rectangles converging to an integral, then briefly reveal the prompt. The animation earns attention before the product pitch.
- Show a correction: circle a cramped label, request a change, and show the verified revision. Record the real process; label sped-up footage and disclose time cuts.
- Show the educator workflow: a tutor describes a common misconception, generates an explanation, and reuses it in a lesson. Focus on the teaching result.
- Invite a specific challenge: ask viewers which concept they want visualized next. Choose one answer and publish a reviewed explanation. Never automatically generate every suggestion.

Use existing product exports for these, with permission where the work belongs to a customer. Avoid a stock-footage montage, invented testimonials, competitor ridicule and fabricated usage numbers.

## Distribution details that matter

YouTube Shorts descriptions and comments do not provide clickable ordinary URLs. Use a channel profile link or the Short's related-video link to a full walkthrough, which then links to Orune. Related-video links require advanced feature access. [YouTube link rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639), [related-video setup](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en).

For search, a later small library of public, reviewed lessons should have a dedicated watch page per subject, a useful explanation/transcript, stable thumbnails and accurate video metadata. A homepage background animation alone is not a substitute for a watch page. Google does not guarantee indexing or video-rich results. [Google video search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video). This branch improves homepage metadata; it does not implement the watch-page library.

Participate in educator communities where the rules allow sharing work. Ask a moderator before posting a promotional demo. Share an explanation that helps the community, describe your relationship to the product, and respond to actual feedback. Do not automate unsolicited DMs.

## Budget and measurement

Start with founder time and a small, explicitly bounded generation allowance. A reasonable first experiment is $50–$100 total in incremental demo compute and assets if needed; this is a proposed cap, not spending authorization. Reuse a good export across formats. Paid advertising should wait until there is a repeatable path from visit to useful video.

Track source → signup → first completed video → download → second completed video → paid subscription. These funnel events are recommendations to instrument; they have not been added by this branch. Use aggregate counts and campaign links, never put customer prompts or emails into campaign parameters. Views are diagnostic; useful videos and repeat users are the outcome.

Judge acquisition against contribution after actual generation, rendering, narration, storage, payment fees and refunds. For example, a $20 subscription with $6 of total variable cost leaves $14 before fixed overhead and acquisition. That is an illustrative scenario, not a measured margin. If variable cost is $12, only $8 remains. Include unsuccessful/free generation costs in the cohort. Do not infer profitability from nominal per-job model limits.

As an initial operating target, aim for acquisition cost recoverable in two months of measured contribution, and stop a test at its pre-agreed budget if it produces no useful activations. Set the actual threshold after seeing cost and retention data. Before spending on promotion, make sure real visitors can sign up, finish an allowed generation, understand credits, and cancel a subscription.
