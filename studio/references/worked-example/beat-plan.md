# Eigenvectors: the directions a transformation preserves

**Format:** 16:9 paper-style Manim lesson, about 39 seconds, four narrated beats.

## Beat 1 — A transformation moves the plane (0–9 s)
- **Claim:** “A linear transformation moves every vector.”
- **Visual:** A faint square grid and axes; two primary-blue vectors from the origin. The grid shears horizontally while the vectors move with it.
- **Teaching purpose:** Establish that a matrix is a geometric action, rather than a table of numbers.
- **Narration:** Introduce the transformation as a machine that changes position and usually direction.

## Beat 2 — Most directions turn (9–18 s)
- **Claim:** “Most vectors change both length and direction.”
- **Visual:** One diagonal vector before and after the shear, with a dashed original direction and a solid transformed direction; the angle between them is visible.
- **Teaching purpose:** Make the common case concrete before naming the exception.
- **Narration:** Point out that the chosen diagonal vector has changed its line of action.

## Beat 3 — Some directions stay on their line (18–29 s)
- **Claim:** “An eigenvector stays on the same line.”
- **Visual:** The horizontal axis-aligned vector is stretched on its existing line. Its original shorter position remains as a pale ghost; its endpoint and label receive the sole accent colour.
- **Teaching purpose:** Identify the invariant direction geometrically.
- **Narration:** Explain that the vector may lengthen, shorten, or reverse, but is still a scaled copy of itself.

## Beat 4 — The eigenvalue says how much (29–40 s)
- **Claim:** “The eigenvalue tells how that line is scaled.”
- **Visual:** Same horizontal vector, now clearly twice as long, beside the composed expression `A v = 2 v`. A small scale bracket marks the extra length.
- **Teaching purpose:** Link the visual scaling to the symbolic eigenvalue equation.
- **Narration:** State that two is the eigenvalue in this example, then summarize eigenvectors as directions that survive the transformation.

## Visual and motion notes
- Warm-paper background; muted running head, black editorial claim, thin untipped axes.
- Primary blue carries vectors and the moving grid. Accent ochre appears once only, at Beat 3’s invariant direction.
- Claims are cross-faded; diagrams visibly deform or transform between beats. No external imagery.
- At each settled beat, run inside-scene and no-overlap guards. Keep the plane and the final expression within the stage band.
